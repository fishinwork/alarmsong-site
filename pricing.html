The small print, in plain words

The things a pricing page usually hides. None of this is a surprise if you read it now.

  • It renews by itself

    Monthly and yearly plans renew automatically until you cancel, and the next period is charged within 24 hours of the current one ending. Cancel at least 24 hours before that, in your store account settings — not in our app, because the store holds the subscription, not us.

  • You pay the store, not us

    Everything goes through the App Store or Google Play. Refunds are theirs to give, under their own rules, and we cannot overrule them.

  • The free days are for subscriptions

    Three of them, on the monthly and yearly plans, once per account. The one-time plan has none — a free trial is a delay before a renewal, and there is nothing to renew.

  • One song a day, on every plan

    Including the one-time one. It is a song every morning, not an unlimited supply of songs — we pay for each one that gets made.

“Forever” means the app’s life, not yours

We would rather write this down now than explain it later.

AlarmSong leans on services we do not own to write the words and make the music. If those change their terms or disappear, or if we cannot keep running this, the songs stop. That is the honest shape of a one-time price on a product that costs money every single morning.

So if that day comes: we tell you at least 30 days ahead, in the app and by email. Anyone who bought the one-time plan in the 12 months before can write to hello@alarmsong.com and ask for their money back.

All of this is in the terms of use, in the same words.

What you get without paying

Right now, all of it — the app is free while it is being built. When the plans open we will say here exactly what the free side keeps, before anything changes, not after.
