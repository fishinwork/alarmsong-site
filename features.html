Features

What it actually does

Everything on this page is in the app today — the wake up song, the styles, the calendar. When something is still being built, it says so.

The music alarm, and the song it plays

  • A hundred styles

    Indie pop, synth-pop, boom bap, phonk, shoegaze, musical theatre, math rock. Search the list, pick a few, and the AI-written song changes style with them, so mornings stop sounding alike.

  • Or your own words

    Nothing in the list fits? Describe the sound you want and it writes to that instead.

  • Sung in your language

    The song does not have to be in English. Pick the language it sings in and the words follow.

  • Words you can read

    The lyrics are there in the app, so the line you half heard at seven in the morning is not lost.

A calendar alarm, not a generic one

It reads tomorrow from the calendar already on your phone — every calendar the phone knows about, nothing to connect one by one. Titles and times, for that morning only, and we do not store them.

Yesterday's unfinished things can come along too, so the song knows what you were avoiding. And if the day is not in any calendar, type it.

The alarm itself

  • A real alarm, not a notification

    It uses the system alarm, so it rings from a locked screen, with the app closed, and plays the song whole rather than the first thirty seconds.

  • A countdown on the lock screen

    A widget that says how long until it rings and what the song is called, without opening anything.

  • Whatever your week looks like

    Every morning, weekdays, weekends, Mondays only — and the week starts on the day your phone says it starts on.

  • A sound to fall back on

    If the song did not arrive, the alarm rings anyway. Silence is the one thing an alarm clock may never do.

The mornings you slept through

Every song goes into a list with the day it belonged to. They are kept for a week — five megabytes each, and a phone is not a warehouse.

Heart the ones worth keeping and they stay for good. Sign in and they survive a new phone; do not sign in and the app still works, it just forgets when the phone does.

And the ordinary things

  • Dark and light

    A warm palette built for seven in the morning, not a white page in the face. It follows whatever your phone is set to.

  • A streak, quietly

    It counts the mornings in a row. It does not nag you about them.

  • Your account, or none

    Sign in with Apple, Google or a code by email — or do not, and use it as it comes. Deleting the account deletes the songs with it.

  • iPhone now, Android next

    AlarmSong needs iOS 26 — that is where an iPhone lets an app ring its own alarm rather than a notification. Android is being built.
